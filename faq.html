Getting in touch

What’s the fastest way to reach Lance?

Phone. Call (209) 986-9292 and you’re speaking with Lance rather than an intake team. If you’d rather write it out, email lance@lancemchan.com or send the quote form — both reach him as well.

Can I just call instead of filling in a form?

Yes, and it’s usually quicker. The form exists for people who’d rather send details at midnight than talk on the phone. Neither is the “proper” channel — use whichever suits you.

What happens after I submit the form?

Your name, contact details and notes go straight to Lance, and he follows up using the phone number or email you supplied. You’ll also see a confirmation on screen so you know it went through.

What should I include in my message?

What you need done, where it is, roughly when you want it handled, and the best way to reach you. If you can add the detail you already know — measurements, access, a previous quote — the follow-up call will be much shorter.

Can I reach Lance outside the working day?

Email lance@lancemchan.com or send the form at any hour — both sit waiting for him without you needing to catch him live. For anything you’d rather discuss, call (209) 986-9292 during the day.

Quotes and pricing

How much will my job cost?

Pricing depends on the specifics, which is why there’s no price list on this site — a published figure would be a guess, and guesses are what make jobs expensive. Call or send your details and Lance will go through the numbers for your job with you.

Can you quote over the phone?

Some jobs can be scoped from a good description; others need more information before any figure is meaningful. On the call Lance will tell you which category yours falls into and what he needs to price it properly.

Am I committing to anything by asking for a quote?

No. A quote is information for you to make a decision with. Take it away, compare it, come back with questions, or don’t — there’s no obligation to proceed.

What’s actually in the quote?

The scope in plain language, what’s included and excluded, anything that could change the figure and why, the order the work would follow, and what’s needed from you before it starts. If a line isn’t clear, call and ask the person who wrote it.

Do you cover my area?

Tell Lance where you are when you get in touch and he’ll confirm whether he can help. Rather than publish a coverage map that may not fit your situation, he’d rather answer the question directly on the call.

What if my job isn’t something you do?

You’ll be told on the first call rather than quoted anyway. A clear “that’s not me” costs you five minutes; a quote from someone who shouldn’t have taken the job costs considerably more.
